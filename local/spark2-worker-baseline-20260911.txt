Node: spark2 (worker), user `reederey`
Method: identical to the head receipts — bf16 8192^3 matmul inside a container
from the same image, `nvidia-smi` sampled every 0.5 s during a sustained load.

WHY
---
The head GPU was pinned at 507 MHz under load while the worker reached ~2400 MHz.
Both nodes run byte-identical software, so a software cause was already
implausible. This captures the worker as a **control** so the head can be compared
against a known-good measurement of the same hardware and the same command.

The head is currently powered off, so its comparison numbers come from the
pre-reboot receipt `spark1-head-clock-post-reboot-20260911.txt`.

IMPORTANT CORRECTION TO AN EARLIER NOTE
---------------------------------------
An intermediate reading in this session showed the worker at "2411 MHz, 12.1 W"
and I described that as the worker's idle state. That was wrong: a subsequent
idle sample set shows **208 MHz / 4.8 W**, matching the head's idle reading of
208 MHz. Both nodes park at 208 MHz when idle, so an idle clock reading tells us
nothing about the fault — exactly as the head receipt already warned. The
discriminator is the clock **under load**, and nothing else.

THE COMPARISON — SAME UTILISATION, 7x THE POWER
-----------------------------------------------
                      spark1 (head, faulty)   spark2 (worker, control)
  clock under load    507 MHz                 2288-2340 MHz
  TFLOP/s             23.8                    86.6
  power under load    ~12.1 W                 74-92 W
  utilisation         96 %                    96 %
  temperature         41-44 C                 62-71 C

Both GPUs report **96 % utilisation**, but the head draws ~12 W where the worker
draws ~88 W. Throughput and clock track power almost exactly:

  clock ratio  2288 / 507   = 4.5x
  power ratio    88 / 12.1  = 7.3x
  speedup       86.6 / 23.8 = 3.6x

A GPU that is 96 % busy yet drawing 12 W is not thermally or thermally-software
limited — it is **starved of power**. This is the strongest evidence yet that the
head's fault is power delivery, not software, and it lines up with the earlier
observation that throughput tracked clock exactly (23.8 x 2431/507 = 114 TFLOP/s),
i.e. the GPU computes correctly and simply never gets the power to raise its clock.

SUPPORTING COUNTER
------------------
`nvidia-smi -q -d PERFORMANCE` accumulated counter, both nodes, same uptime order:

  head   SW Power Capping: 110034847 us  (~110 s)
  worker SW Power Capping:   4000187 us  (~4 s)

The head has accumulated ~27x more power-capping time than the worker. Treat with
caution: it is not established from this data whether the counter resets at driver
load or accumulates over the machine's life, so it is corroborating rather than
conclusive. It is recorded because it points the same way as the power comparison.

WHAT REMAINS UNPROVEN
---------------------
This does not identify *why* the head is power-starved. Candidates, none
established: an under-delivering 240 W USB-C PD supply or cable; a PD negotiation
failure; a failing VRM or power stage on the head's board; or a firmware power
limit that is not exposed through the standard interfaces. GB10 exposes **no**
`/sys/class/power_supply` class on either node, and `nvidia-smi` reports
Current/Requested/Default/Min/Max Power Limit as **N/A** on both, so the supply
and any PD contract cannot be inspected from software. That is why this needs
physical access.

Also unresolved: whether the head's hard power-off during model load is the same
fault progressing. It is consistent with a marginal supply collapsing under a
sustained high-power demand (model load drives both GPU and memory hard), but that
is a hypothesis, not a measurement.
